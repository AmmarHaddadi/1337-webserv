In June 1914, Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria was assassinated by a Serbian nationalist. This local crisis triggered a chain reaction of secret military alliances across Europe. Within weeks, the continent split into two massive opposing sides.The Western Front quickly turned into a brutal stalemate defined by trench warfare. Millions of soldiers lived in muddy, rat-infested ditches, facing machine guns and poison gas. For four years, neither side could make a major breakthrough.The war finally ended on November 11, 1918, when Germany signed an armistice. By then, four major empires had collapsed, and over 20 million people were dead.
